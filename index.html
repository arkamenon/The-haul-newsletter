Fleet demand stays strong despite July slowdown in Class 8 orders

The 2026 Class 8 market is effectively sold out. July’s 30% month-over-month drop in orders is strictly a supply issue—a lack of available production slots—not a drop in demand. If clients need equipment, they must pivot strategies to 2027 models, medium-duty, or the used market.

Despite the monthly dip, July orders (~22,000 units) were up roughly 70% year-over-year. Year-to-date orders are up 120%. With 2026 capacity maxed out, fleets are waiting on 2027 order books to open. Manufacturers are currently holding off due to uncertainty around upcoming EPA emissions regulations.

As new inventory stalls, the used market is absorbing the demand. Used Class 8 retail sales jumped 9.6% last month, driving average retail prices up to $61,751. Classes 5–7 are seeing massive growth, with July orders up 41% year-over-year (18,300 units), proving that overall freight fundamentals remain strong.

The SMB shipping trap: Trying to go it alone

Small and midsize business (SMB) clients are currently getting squeezed by rising shipping costs—from fluctuating fuel and tariffs to sudden carrier rate updates—without the dedicated logistics or compliance teams that enterprise shippers rely on. The single most expensive trap these SMBs fall into is trying to navigate this complex landscape in-house. Crucially, SMBs are not simply “shrunken enterprises”; enterprise tactics like stockpiling inventory to dodge tariffs tie up critical cash flow, while splitting volumes across regional carriers often backfires. While SMBs lack enterprise capital, their main competitive edge is speed and agility, meaning they need flexible, right-sized shipping strategies rather than generic enterprise playbooks.

Capacity Pullback Keeps Freight Spot Rates Near Historical Highs

Capacity is pulling back faster than freight volumes across the board. This dynamic is tightening load-to-truck ratios and driving up spot rates, causing them to invert (or nearly invert) their historical relationship with contract rates.

  • Dry VanSpot linehaul averaged $2.32 per mile, down 2.4%, or $0.06 per mile, week over week and up 42.1%, or $0.68 per mile, year over year, within the five-year range of $1.53 to $2.90.
  • ReeferSpot linehaul averaged $2.65 per mile, down 2.6%, or $0.07 per mile, week over week and up 37.7%, or $0.73 per mile, year over year, within the five-year range of $1.82 to $3.33.
  • FlatbedSpot linehaul averaged $2.83 per mile, down 1.5%, or $0.04 per mile, week over week and up 39.6%, or $0.81 per mile, year over year, within the five-year range of $1.83 to $3.01.

US manufacturing expands for seventh month in a row

The U.S. manufacturing sector saw robust growth in July, expanding for the seventh consecutive month with a PMI of 55.6% (up 2.3 points from June). The overall U.S. economy also marked its 21st consecutive month of growth. While demand and production are surging, manufacturers are continuing to navigate pricing volatility and geopolitical supply chain risks.

The Production Index surged 6.3 points to 58.5%, hitting its highest level since November 2021. The New Orders Index also grew for the seventh straight month, reaching 56.7%.

The Employment Index returned to expansion territory for the first time in 33 months, climbing to 52.8% (up 3.1 points) as the sector added 3,000 jobs.
